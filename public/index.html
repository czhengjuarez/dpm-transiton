The role is changing. Decide what it becomes.

Design program management is a bundle of work that used to travel together. The bundle came apart. Some of it moves to a TPM, some of it a tool now does, some of it should stop being produced, and some of it becomes the most valuable thing the role does.

Below are 38 tasks, taken from eight real Design Program Manager job postings. Make five kinds of call on each one. At the end you get the role rewritten, and a plan for the person holding it.

What this did not measure

It counts tasks, not hours. That cuts against the whole exercise. Automating seventy percent of a role's tasks removes very little of the role if the remaining thirty percent is where the week actually goes, and nobody has published what share of a DPM's time goes to judgment work.

So run it, then open the calendar for the same week and compare. If the two disagree, the calendar is right.
